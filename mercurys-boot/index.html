Mercurys Boot & the Stargate Tunnel

An off-track loop along the Capertee cliffline: a boot-shaped rock high above the valley, an old Aboriginal route through the cliffs, and a hidden sandstone tunnel.

Region
Capertee Valley rim, near Ben Bullen
Walk type
Off-track loop
Highlights
Mercurys Boot, Baal Bone Gap, Stargate Tunnel

High above the Capertee Valley, a great sandstone block leans out over the drop. From the right angle it looks just like a boot. This loop takes you along the escarpment to Mercurys Boot, down through Baal Bone Gap and on to McLeans Pass, where a triangular tunnel cuts straight through the rock.

01

Getting there

The aim is to park as close to Baal Bone Gap as you can. How close you get depends on your vehicle and how much risk you're happy to take on the fire trails.

From Mudgee

Moffitts Trail

Head for Moffitts Trail (also signposted as Ben Bullen Trail). Conventional vehicles should stop wherever the track starts to look too rough for them.

Four-wheel drives can usually get to a sharp corner and steep descent about 1 km before the junction with the Bicentennial National Trail. Don't drive down unless you're confident you can drive back up.

From the south

Unnamed junction

There's a good place to park at an unnamed junction a couple of kilometres south of Baal Bone Creek.

Fire trail conditions change quickly after rain. Check the current NPWS alerts for Gardens of Stone National Park before you go.

02

The walk

The route has no formed track. Expect scrub, rock platforms and some route-finding, with the cliffline as your guide.

  1. 1 Start

    To the Capertee cliffline

    Leave Moffitts Trail and head cross-country to the Capertee cliffline. When you reach the cliff edge, follow it down towards the valley. The escarpment guides you most of the way, and the views open up over the Capertee Valley as you go.

  2. 2 Highlight

    Mercurys Boot

    Keep going along the cliffline until you reach Mercurys Boot. You'll know it when you see it: a big rock jutting out over the valley, shaped unmistakably like a boot. It's a great place for a break and photos.

  3. 3 Through the cliffs

    Baal Bone Gap

    Just below the Boot is Baal Bone Gap, a natural break in the cliffs and an easy way down into the valley. You may find water in one of the pipes at the Gap, but the tap may now be locked, so don't rely on it.

  4. 4 About 1 km east

    McLeans Pass

    From Baal Bone Gap, head east, roughly following the cliffline for a kilometre or so, until you reach McLeans Pass. There's a bit of a drop to get into the pass.

  5. 5 Highlight

    Stargate Tunnel

    Once you're at the bottom of the pass, head a little south to the Stargate Tunnel. This triangular sandstone tunnel is one of the highlights of the walk.

    Length ~20 m Width ~7 m Shape Triangular
  6. 6 Finish

    Back along the ridge

    From the Stargate Tunnel, climb back up to the ridgeline and follow it south until you reach the unnamed road. From there, the fire trails lead back to the Bicentennial National Trail and your car.

03

Before you go

Off-track navigation

This walk isn't signposted or marked. Carry a map and compass or GPS, and know how to use them.

Cliff edges

The cliffs are high and sheer, and the edges can be undercut. Keep well back, especially with children or in wind.

Water

Don't count on finding any. The tap at Baal Bone Gap may be locked, so carry all you'll need, particularly in summer.

Respect the country

Baal Bone Gap has Aboriginal cultural sites, including hand stencils. Look, but don't touch. Leave no trace.

Tell someone

Leave your plans with someone, and think about carrying a PLB. There's very little phone coverage out there.
